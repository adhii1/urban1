\section{Executive summary}
TORQQ supports recurring commute service and on-demand Flexy operations with Node.js/Express, MongoDB/Mongoose, and real-time driver workflows. Current recurring service uses plans, subscriptions, routes, daily route/date trips, and passenger manifests. Current on-demand service uses ride requests and, when rides are bundled, a ShuttleSession with passenger-specific lifecycle state.

\textbf{Payment status at a glance.} The backend supports Razorpay order creation, Razorpay signature verification, and Razorpay refunds for the implemented purchase and completed-ride flows. UPI is \status{Provider-configured}: it is available only when Razorpay Checkout and the merchant's Razorpay account/dashboard configuration permit it. TORQQ does not independently implement or guarantee UPI availability.

\textbf{Wallet and Store Balance status.} They are \status{Not implemented} as stored-value payment methods. Current wallet routes expose reward, referral, and refund information only. This repository has no stored-value wallet/Store Balance ledger and no debit, top-up, settlement, or reconciliation API. Customers therefore cannot select Wallet or Store Balance as a payment method today.

\subsection{UPI: provider-controlled, not a TORQQ-specific payment rail}
UPI availability is controlled through Razorpay Checkout and requires the applicable merchant/account/dashboard enablement. The server-side order and signature verification capabilities are prerequisites for a Checkout flow, but they do not prove that UPI is enabled, visible to every customer, or approved for a production account. Validate UPI in the intended Razorpay account/dashboard and production Checkout configuration before communicating availability to customers.

\subsection{Wallet and Store Balance: explicit non-implementation}
The following current routes are informational only: \code{GET /api/v1/wallet/rewards}, \code{GET /api/v1/wallet/referrals}, and \code{GET /api/v1/wallet/refunds}. They must not be interpreted as a wallet, Store Balance, or payment instrument.

\textbf{No stored-value financial capability is present in the reviewed repository:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item no wallet or Store Balance collection/model;
  \item no immutable credit/debit ledger entries or balance calculation;
  \item no top-up, payment debit, authorization hold, release, reversal, or refund-to-balance operation;
  \item no settlement, provider reconciliation, idempotency-key, or financial audit workflow; and
  \item no API that lets a customer select Wallet or Store Balance at checkout.
\end{itemize}

\section{End-to-end operations}
\subsection{Customer authentication and recurring subscription flow}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The customer authenticates and accesses plans and eligible active routes.
  \item For a recurring non-Flexy purchase, the customer supplies the plan, route, start date, required weekday choices, and pickup/drop stop selections.
  \item The backend validates the plan/service model, active route, distinct forward-ordered managed stops, and policy rules. Hybrid requires exactly three distinct Monday--Friday selections. Managed Home-to-Office validation applies the server-side 5 km stop-distance threshold.
  \item Only after policy acceptance does \code{POST /api/v1/customer/subscriptions/purchase} persist a \code{PENDING\_PAYMENT} Subscription and create a Razorpay order.
  \item The customer completes Razorpay Checkout. The client submits the returned order identifier, payment identifier, and signature to \code{POST /api/v1/customer/subscriptions/verify-payment}.
  \item On verified signature, the Subscription becomes \code{ACTIVE}, payment metadata is recorded, and the Customer's \code{subscriptionId} is linked. For shared recurring plans, route/date trips and manifest entries are generated.
  \item The daily generator continues to prepare the next local service day. Eligibility respects subscription dates, active status, tier calendar rules, durable stops, and route state.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Flexy immediate and scheduled ride flow}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A customer selecting a Flexy plan is directed to the on-demand booking path rather than a recurring Subscription purchase.
  \item The server receives pickup and drop GeoJSON points and an explicit pickup intent.
  \item A valid \code{IMMEDIATE} request is created as a RideRequest with status \code{PENDING}; matching may begin.
  \item A valid future \code{SCHEDULED} request stores \code{scheduledPickupAt} and status \code{SCHEDULED}. A timestamp is required for scheduled requests and prohibited for immediate requests.
  \item The scheduled Flexy promotion job runs on an interval. It conditionally promotes each due \code{SCHEDULED} request to \code{PENDING} once, then invokes ordinary matching for the rides it promoted.
  \item The accepted ride advances through \code{ACCEPTED}, \code{DRIVER\_ARRIVING}, \code{IN\_PROGRESS}, and terminal \code{COMPLETED}, \code{CANCELLED}, or \code{EXPIRED} states as applicable. Bundled rides additionally use a ShuttleSession and passenger-specific lifecycle.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Ride payment and refund flow}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item After a ride is \code{COMPLETED} and has a final or estimated fare, the authenticated customer requests \code{POST /api/v1/payments/create-order}.
  \item The backend creates an INR Razorpay order and records the order identifier, fare amount, and payment status \code{pending} on the RideRequest.
  \item Razorpay Checkout returns the order identifier, payment identifier, and signature; the client submits them to \code{POST /api/v1/payments/verify}.
  \item The backend verifies the signature. It records payment \code{completed} after successful verification or \code{failed} after an unsuccessful verification.
  \item For an eligible completed or cancelled ride that has a completed payment and no earlier refund, the customer calls \code{POST /api/v1/payments/refund}. The backend requests the Razorpay refund and stores the refund identifier, status, amount, reason, and timestamp.
  \item \code{GET /api/v1/payments/:rideId/status} returns the persisted fare and payment record. This is Razorpay-backed payment status, not wallet balance status.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Driver online, acceptance, and passenger lifecycle}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item An approved active Driver is assigned a User identity, vehicle/document record, route assignment where applicable, current GeoJSON location, and online/availability state.
  \item For on-demand matching, drivers are identified using availability and location. A RideRequest retains the matched-driver responses and the accepted driver reference.
  \item On acceptance of a bundled set, the backend creates one ShuttleSession and persists that \code{shuttleSessionId} on every accepted RideRequest before acknowledgement. The session is the aggregate work record; each RideRequest remains the authoritative passenger-specific lifecycle record.
  \item Each passenger has a distinct OTP, pickup, drop, and \code{PENDING} passenger lifecycle. Driver pickup verification targets both the ShuttleSession and RideRequest, validates ownership and OTP/predecessor state, and advances only the selected passenger to \code{BOARDED}.
  \item Driver drop completion similarly targets the same session and ride, advances only a \code{BOARDED} passenger to \code{DROPPED}, and records timing. The ShuttleSession becomes \code{COMPLETED} only when every passenger is dropped.
  \item For recurring trips, drivers receive a route/date Trip with a manifest of customer, selected stop snapshots, and passenger lifecycle state; accepted shared recurring work is not an on-demand acceptance offer.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scheduled commute-trip generation}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The daily job computes the next local service date and invokes the trip generator.
  \item The generator finds active, non-deleted, eligible recurring subscriptions and groups them by active route.
  \item It creates or updates one \code{SCHEDULED} Trip for each route/service-date group and adds one \code{PENDING} manifest entry per eligible subscription.
  \item The manifest copies the selected durable pickup/drop stop snapshots so the scheduled work remains traceable even when route arrays are later reordered.
  \item A configured active route driver is copied to the Trip. If no active assigned driver exists, the Trip is unassigned and an \code{UNASSIGNED\_DRIVER} OperationalException is created. A configured-driver assignment failure is reported as \code{DRIVER\_ASSIGNMENT\_FAILED}.
  \item Re-running the generator for the same active route and normalized service date preserves one Trip and one manifest entry per eligible subscription through route/date idempotency.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Admin route changes, reconciliation, and exceptions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item An administrator manages plans, customers, drivers, routes, subscriptions, and trips according to their role and permissions.
  \item A driver-only route change updates future non-terminal scheduled Trips. Completed and cancelled Trips are not changed.
  \item Route deactivation, stop removal, or stop reordering is reconciled against durable stop identifiers and manifest snapshots. Affected future entries are marked \code{REQUIRES\_RESOLUTION}; no silent stop substitution is made.
  \item The system records an OperationalException with route/trip/subscription references, service date, reason, status, and resolution information.
  \item An administrator resolves a conflict only by selecting valid replacement forward stops. The replacement is propagated to the Subscription and future non-terminal Trip manifests.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Embedded structures and lifecycle controls}
\textbf{Durable stops.} Route stops have a stable \code{stopId} rather than relying on array position. Subscriptions store durable selected IDs and sequence snapshots. Trip manifests preserve a stop snapshot containing ID, name, sequence order, and GeoJSON location. This makes route reconciliation deterministic when stops are reordered.

\textbf{Trip manifest.} Each embedded manifest entry references the customer and subscription, carries pickup/drop stop snapshots, and has status \code{PENDING|BOARDED|DROPPED|NO\_SHOW}. A conflict subdocument records \code{NONE} or \code{REQUIRES\_RESOLUTION}, reason, and detection time. It also holds boarding/drop timestamps.

\textbf{Soft delete.} User, Customer, Driver, Admin, Plan, Subscription, Route, Trip, RideRequest, and ShuttleSession use \code{isDeleted} fields in the reviewed models. Query middleware filters deleted records for the models where defined; operational workflows should preserve this behavior rather than relying on client filtering.

\textbf{GeoJSON convention.} Customer locations, driver location, route-stop locations, RideRequest endpoints/stops, Trip stop snapshots, and ShuttleSession sequence locations use GeoJSON \code{Point} structures. Coordinates are ordered \code{[longitude, latitude]}, not latitude/longitude.

\section{Operational controls and handover notes}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Treat a verified Razorpay signature as the application activation condition for a pending subscription; do not activate solely because a client reports success.
  \item Monitor daily trip generation summaries and OperationalException records, especially unassigned routes, configured-driver assignment failures, and route conflicts.
  \item Preserve route stop IDs during route maintenance. Reordering/deleting stops requires reconciliation; it must not silently remap subscriptions by array index.
  \item Keep RideRequest passenger lifecycle authoritative for bundled rides. Session-level status does not replace the per-passenger state used for safe pickup/drop actions.
  \item Do not expose payment provider credentials, OTP values, or payment signatures in logs or client support artifacts.
  \item Before production payment launch, validate configured credentials, actual provider mode, error/retry policy, refunds, webhook/settlement needs, and account-specific UPI availability.
\end{itemize}

\section{Client FAQ}
\textbf{Is Store Balance completed?} No. Store Balance is \status{Not implemented} in this repository. There is no ledger, top-up/debit/settlement API, or customer payment selection.

\textbf{Is Wallet completed?} No, not as a payment method. The current backend exposes reward, referral, and refund information routes only. Those routes do not create or maintain a stored-value balance.

\textbf{Is UPI completed?} TORQQ has backend Razorpay order creation and signature verification support. UPI itself is \status{Provider-configured} through Razorpay Checkout and requires merchant dashboard/account enablement and validation. It must not be represented as guaranteed until confirmed in the production provider configuration.

\textbf{Can customers pay with Wallet or Store Balance now?} No. A customer-facing selection flow does not exist, and the required stored-value backend controls are not present.

\textbf{Do we need frontend work now to prepare a future wallet?} No frontend change is needed merely to design backend contracts or keep the capability feature-gated. A frontend change is required before customers can select Wallet or Store Balance.

\textbf{What payment operations are implemented now?} Razorpay-backed order creation, signature verification, and refunds for the implemented subscription and completed-ride flows, subject to configured credentials and normal production readiness checks.

\textbf{Does this guide certify production readiness?} No. It documents implementation and integration status as of August 19, 2026; it is not a production certification.
